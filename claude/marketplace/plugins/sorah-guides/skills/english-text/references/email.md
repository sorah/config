# Business Email

Conventions for English business email in the author's voice, from sponsorship-desk correspondence (299 emails, 2018–2026). For RubyKaigi bulk broadcasts (Dear/Attn lines, `----` separators, `Sorah on behalf of Team RubyKaigi`), follow rubykaigi-english-writing first; this file covers the voice underneath.

The voice has shifted over the years: 2018–2020 mail was chattier (named greetings, manual sign-offs, ellipses, "Sorry for being late"); 2024–2026 mail is terser and more structured. Target the current register unless the conversation calls for warmth.

Examples are lightly corrected for grammar; vary wording rather than copying.

## Shape

1. Greeting:
   - None for quick replies and follow-ups within a thread.
   - `Hi,` as the default for a new reply.
   - `Hi <Name>,` with an established individual contact.
   - `Hello,` for bad news, refusals, or delayed replies; `Hello <Name>,` for formal first contact.
   - Never `Dear <Name>,` in a 1:1 email.
2. Optional self-intro on first contact: `This is Sorah from Team RubyKaigi.` / `I'm Sorah from RubyKaigi organisers.`, often followed by `Thank you for sponsoring us!`
3. Body: answer first. Paragraphs of 1–3 sentences.
4. Optional closing line or one warm sentence. A manual `Thanks,` / `Best,` + `Sorah` above the signature is fine.
5. Signature (when the mail client doesn't insert it):

```
—
Sorah Fukumori
Team RubyKaigi
https://rubykaigi.org/
```

## Answering Multiple Questions

Pick one layout per message:

Quote then answer:

```
> Can our table cover be used at RubyKaigi this year?

Yes, we don't have such branding this year so you can use your own table cover.
```

Topic: answer lines:

```
Setup date: Covered in the exhibitor guide.
Number of attendees: We currently have 1,300 attendees, excluding booth staff.
Point of contact: Please come to the reception. You may request escalation to English-speaking staff members as needed.
```

Numbered answers mirroring the sender's numbering:

```
1. Yes. Either ticket is sufficient.
2. Possible, but as for (1), it wastes your assigned passes.
3. Affirm.
4. True.
```

Lists are introduced by a colon-ended lead-in: `Please find the following prices:`, `2 concerns:`.

Contract and agreement reviews go point by point with clause numbers: `(2) The term "the Hotel" needs to be replaced with "the Venue", as our venue is not a hotel.` Quote the clause, then state the issue (`I find the following sentence unclear:`). Spell out the legal entity and signatory when relevant.

## Phrase Palette

Acknowledging: `Noted.` · `Thank you, noted.` · `Understood, we'll get back to you after internal discussion.` · `Affirm, as written in the prospectus.` · `All acknowledged, looks fine to us.` · `Thanks, confirmed the payment!` · `Thanks, refunded as requested.` · `Gotcha, thanks for the confirmation!`

Interjections (sparingly, in replies to known contacts): `Oops, I hadn't double-checked the status before I wrote the earlier email.` · `Ah, I see.` · `Mm. Okay.`

Holding: `Still confirming details, we appreciate your patience.` · `I'm checking with our vendor.` · `Let me check with my colleagues.` · `Standby, will be available in early March.` · `Please stand by until they complete inspection of returned items.`

Thanks for waiting: `Thank you for the patience.` · `We again appreciate your patience.` · `Thank you for the heads-up.`

Requests: `Could you prepare a draft and share it with us?` · `Could you elaborate on your situation, including which tickets you wish to have refunded?` · `Could you check the status once again?` · `Please let us know your intention.` · `Let me know …` · `I'd suggest updating our contact information to sponsorships@rubykaigi.org.` · `I'd recommend procuring one independently and bringing your own.`

Apology (one clause + cause): `Sorry for the late reply due to Japanese bank holidays.` · `Apologies for the delayed response - I was out at my company's offsite.` · `Oops. My apologies: 4 conference passes for Platinum sponsors.` · `Disregard this, and sorry for the confusion.`

Citing documents: `As stated in our prospectus, …` · `See also: FAQ [When will you send the invoice?] on the prospectus: <url>` · `All information above is provided at <url>, so we kindly request you to review it carefully.`

Alternatives: `If it does not work, we'll try to issue a letter.` · `An alternative option would be connecting our vendor and your local partner.` · `We can ask our vendor, but please expect it is a last-resort option.`

Hedging (light, factual): `I think we're waiting for your member.` · `I believe the request is likely legit.` · `Should be fine, but most sponsors tend to use a table cloth.` · `No guarantee, but we typically respond to questions quickly.` · `(to be confirmed)` · `on a best-effort basis`

Availability and time zones: `We're in JST, but I'm in ET on 16 Mar.` · `Since it's already night in JST, …` · `It'll be updated in 6-8 hours as it's midnight JST now.`

Warmth (one line, usually at the end): `Thanks as always for your continued support!` · `Thank you for your interest in sponsoring us!` · `Awesome, I'm looking forward to it!` · `See you at the Kaigi!` · `Happy holidays,`

Closing: `Thanks,` · `Best,` · `Cheers,` · `Thank you for your understanding and cooperation.`

## Refusals & Policy

Verdict → reason (cite the policy or physical constraint) → optional alternative/future plan → one-line thanks. State policy as fact; ask for understanding, not forgiveness.

- "Unfortunately it's not possible for RubyKaigi 2024. As we have large demand for booth space, we tried to accommodate everyone with a booth. We are considering plans to improve this situation by reviewing add-on pricing. Thank you for the feedback!"
- "No, due to privacy reasons and because our lanyards don't have RFID or barcodes. As this is our policy, we don't plan to have such systems in future Kaigis either."
- "Because our sponsorship programme runs with no cancellation policy after finalisation, we'll remove you from the booth list, but the booth add-on is non-cancellable and non-refundable. We appreciate your understanding regarding this policy."
- "We don't accept cancellation, withdrawal, or changes to a selected package once your sponsorship is finalised."
- "Thank you for the proposal, but we have no interest in using your product for check-in."
- "We can't provide legal advice."

Formal bad news (postponements, selection results, closed applications) may use `Unfortunately, we regret to inform you that …` or `I'm afraid we cannot …`. Keep these for genuinely bad news, not routine refusals.

Context to justify limits: "we are a community-run conference where most staff members are volunteers"; "our vendor is less capable of English, so it is considered a last-resort option."

Security reasoning is stated briefly in parentheses: `(I can't proceed with this from your address because I can't confirm <Name>'s intention. The request is likely legit, but just in case, for security.)`

## Reminders & Correcting Behaviour

Blunt fact, then a choice:

- "We haven't heard back from you for 2 weeks. Do you want to continue with the normal sponsorship package (Gold) as submitted for now? Otherwise, we accept withdrawal of your application if you wish to cancel. Please let us know your intention."
- Follow-up: "We have cancelled your sponsorship application as we haven't heard back."
- "Updates? We're still waiting for the DocuSign request."
- "Repeating, I need the email address used for your RubyKaigi registration."
- "Hi, I'd request you to always avoid using personal contacts, to ensure visibility of conversations across our team."
- "Please remember most changes can be self-served from https://sponsorships.rubykaigi.org/, which saves both our time and yours."
- "I'd appreciate it if you could review it once before asking us a question."
- "Our event name is RubyKaigi, not Ruby Kaigi."

## Subject Lines

- Conference tag first: `[RubyKaigi 2026] W-9 form`, `[RubyKaigi 2025] Contract`. Generic `[RubyKaigi]` when cross-year; edition names as-is (`[RubyKaigi Takeout 2021]`).
- Extra tags stacked without spaces after the conference tag: `[RubyKaigi 2026][URGENT][ACTION REQUIRED] Pick up swag roller case by 18:30`. Other tags: `[IMPORTANT]`, `[WARNING]`.
- Rest is a terse noun phrase, or a full statement when the subject is the message: `[RubyKaigi 2026] Exhibitor tickets are ineligible for social events`, `[RubyKaigi 2025][WARNING] We don't accept inquiries via phone calls`.
- Revisions dated in parentheses: `Exhibitors' guide: Updated (13 Mar)`.

## Thank-you / Wrap-up Notes

Concrete numbers, then gratitude: `We welcomed 1,434 attendees (including booth passes) from 39 countries and regions.` · `We couldn't have done it without your support.`

## Formats

- Dates: `12 Mar 2026`, or `12 Mar` when the year is obvious; `21 Apr (Tue)`. No ordinals (`16th`). Deadlines with time zone: `8 Sep 2021, 10:00 UTC (19:00 JST)`.
- Time ranges: `4pm-6pm`, `9:30-10:00`. 24-hour times in notices (`17:00`).
- Money: `25,500 JPY`, `2,500,000 JPY (tax excl.)`.
- UI labels in brackets: `[Void] button`, `[change] or [re-assign] link`.
- `^` may point back to the preceding item: `for additional equipment ^, please confirm …`.
